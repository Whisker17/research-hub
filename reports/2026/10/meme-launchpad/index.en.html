Meme Launchpad: Mechanics, Evolution & Agentic Design

Launchpads make contracts the trading counterparty so token launches require zero upfront capital; each generation primarily evolved to eliminate migration attack vectors. Agentic designs require verifiable on-chain permissions.

typeTechnical Research · OnboardingaudienceDevelopers familiar with EVM / Solidity exploring meme launchpads and agent wallets for the first timescopeMechanics and lifecycle, major product post-mortems, design rationale for Mantle's agent-first launchpadevidence184 claims ledger (observed 133 / inferred 34 / speculative 17), primary sources accessed 2026-10-05date2026-10-05 · v1

Bottom line: Meme launchpads use contracts as trading counterparties, pricing buys and sells on constant-product bonding curves with virtual reserves. This lets creators launch without supplying initial liquidity, and no channel exists to withdraw liquidity during the curve phase. Successive designs have reduced the attack surface at migration upon graduation (F1). Opening sniping, activity collapse after subsidies expire, and per-token value dilution remain unresolved (F2, F5, F6). Bankr already enables agents to launch tokens; what agentic architectures must provide is verifiable permissions: implemented as an on-chain verifiable session key sandbox (F8).

Conclusions

Master the curve and graduation before discussing agents
  1. Mechanics: Contracts use virtual reserves as counterparties, enabling zero upfront capital for creators; burning or permanently locking LP positions prevents withdrawal of that liquidity, making liquidity-withdrawal rug pulls technically impossible. 2) Lessons: Relocating funds into external pools at graduation is the single largest engineering risk; subsidies cannot purchase retention. 3) Agentic: The missing layer is permissioning, not the underlying chain; start on Mantle L2 with application-layer account abstraction, and only explore L3 if conditions F1–F3 are empirically proven on-chain.

Background & Motivation

Problem: Launching a new token on traditional DEXs requires creators to provide both sides of the initial liquidity while retaining the ability to withdraw it at will. Retail users fear rugs, while bots snipe the first block after launch. Launchpads make the contract serve as the trading counterparty, converting these three trust problems into deterministic code.

Why Now:

  • Two attempts on Mantle have ceased operations: Funny Money (launched 2025-02 as an AI agent launchpad and DeFAI terminal) and Printr (multi-chain launchpad, ceased operations 2026-08) [23][24].
  • Agentic token issuance and trading are already operational on Base and Robinhood Chain (Bankr, Clanker) [25][13].
  • Mantle is planning an agent-first launchpad, requiring technical documentation for developers to build upon.

Scope: Mechanics and mathematics, complete lifecycle, retrospective analysis of major products, structural flaws, and architectural foundations for agentic designs.

Non-goals: Tape (xStocks-denominated launchpad proposals); compliance details; schedules and KPIs; financial advice.

Running Example: The classic pump.fun SOL curve. Its parameters are public and reproducible; every subsequent panel illustrates mechanics on this curve before generalizing.

Prerequisites

B-0

Constant Product AMM

~5min
What it is

A liquidity pool holding assets x and y, maintaining x × y = k before and after trades. Purchasing more y increases the price of remaining y.

Why it matters here

Launchpad curves implement this formula, substituting part of the reserves with "virtual" numbers.

Minimal example
Pool: x = 10 SOL, y = 1000 tokens, k = 10000
Buy with 1 SOL → x = 11, y = 10000/11 ≈ 909.1, receive ~90.9 tokens
Common misconception

Misconception: Prices are set by manual order books. Fact: Price is strictly determined by the ratio of the two reserves.

B-1

Virtual-Reserve Bonding Curve

~8min
What it is

Adding constants to both sides of the constant product formula to act as non-existent "virtual" reserves. The contract prices each buy and sell along this curve.

Why it matters here

Under pure real reserves, initial SOL reserve is 0, giving an initial price of 0 where the first buy could drain the pool virtually for free; conversely, the price of the last token tends toward infinity. Virtual reserves resolve both challenges simultaneously.

Minimal example
pump.fun: (x + 30) × (y + 2.799e8) = 3.219e10
Opening price: 30 / 1.073e9 ≈ 2.80e-8 SOL
Common misconception

Misconception: Virtual reserves represent capital subsidized by the platform. Fact: They are immutable constants within the formula; no party can withdraw them.

B-2

Concentrated and Single-Sided Liquidity

~6min
What it is

Concentrated liquidity restricts LP market making to a bounded price range. Single-sided liquidity places the entire position on one side of current price, holding only a single asset.

Why it matters here

Clanker, Zora, and PAIR deploy their entire token supply as a single-sided position, absorbing quote assets as price appreciates. This position replicates bonding curve behavior without requiring graduation.

Common misconception

Misconception: Single-sided pools have an active counterparty at launch. Fact: The pool initially holds only the new token; the first buyer experiences zero competitive liquidity.

B-3

Uniswap v4 Hooks

~8min
What it is

Contracts attached at pool initialization invoked before and after swaps or liquidity modifications. They can dynamically alter swap amounts and fee tiers.

Why it matters here

Decaying taxes, maximum wallet caps, fee routing, and in-pool graduation are implemented via hooks. All pools are managed by a single PoolManager, making pool creation a single state update.

Minimal example
The lower 14 bits of hook address define permissions:
beforeSwap = 1 << 7
beforeSwapReturnDelta = 1 << 3
Common misconception

Misconception: A pool's hook address can be replaced after initialization. Fact: Hook addresses are embedded in pool IDs and remain immutable.

B-4

Sniping and MEV

~5min
What it is

Sniping occurs when automated bots purchase tokens in the first block after launch. MEV represents value extracted via transaction ordering, including sandwich attacks.

Why it matters here

Virtual reserve curves are heavily front-loaded: upon 30 SOL net inflow, 53.65% of total supply has already been distributed. Earlier purchases capture disproportionately larger supply fractions.

Common misconception

Misconception: Sniping is a governance flaw solvable by banning bots. Fact: It stems directly from curve geometry; protocols can only mitigate it via delays, decaying taxes, and wallet limits.

Go deeper

[13] Clanker MEV Module Documentation

B-5

Account Abstraction and Session Keys

~8min
What it is

Account abstraction makes accounts smart contracts, enabling custom signature verification. session key delegations are temporary keys authorized for specific contracts, functions, allowances, and expiries.

Why it matters here

Autonomous agents must launch and trade tokens without possessing the master root private key. EIP-7702 allows EOAs to delegate to smart contract code; ERC-4337 provides gas sponsorship via Paymaster and 2D nonce parallel channels.

Common misconception

Misconception: EIP-7702 inherently enforces spend limits and whitelists. Fact: 7702 only delegates execution; spend control relies on the delegation contract or ERC-7579 modules.

Token Lifecycle: Creation to External Market

Curve Pricing Schedule (Classic pump.fun SOL curve, derived via formula [1]):

Net Inflow (SOL) Sold % of Total Supply Token Price (SOL) Multiple vs Opening
0 0% 2.80e-8 1×
10 26.82% 4.97e-8 1.78×
30 53.65% 1.12e-7 4.0×
60 71.53% 2.52e-7 9.0×
85.0 (Graduation) 79.31% 4.11e-7 14.7×

The first 30 SOL captures over half of the entire supply, whereas the final 25 SOL distributes only ~7.8%. This mathematical asymmetry explains why opening sniping is structurally lucrative (B-4).

Key Observations Across Lifecycle Stages:

  1. Creation: Zero protocol creation fee [2]. Token initialization and initial purchase can be bundled in a single transaction as a dev buy [4].
  2. Curve Phase: Fixed 1.25% fee per trade (0.30% creator, 0.95% protocol) [2]. The base contract has no anti-sniping protection beyond user slippage settings max_sol_cost / min_sol_output [1].
  3. Graduation Gate: Triggered during a buy instruction when real_token_reserves == 0 is reached [1]. The commonly cited "$69k graduation" simply reflects 410.88 SOL market capitalization at contemporary SOL valuations.
  4. Migration: migrate is permissionless and idempotent; anyone may trigger it [1]. Early architectures relayed funds to Raydium via an off-chain server using a privileged withdraw instruction (now disabled) [1]. Since 2025-03-20, migration routes into PumpSwap, dropping migration fees from 6 SOL to 0 [5].
  5. AMM Phase: Graduated canonical poolss apply tiered trading fees based on market capitalization [2][4].
  6. The Long Tail: The overwhelming majority of tokens stall on the curve or depreciate to zero post-graduation. Under Uniswap v4, creating a pool requires only a storage update within the PoolManager singleton without deploying separate contracts [9].
CAUTION: Modern pump.fun Diverges from Classic Parameters

The source author reported September 2026 samples injecting only 0.086 SOL with supplies deviating from 1.0B tokens. Mayhem mode, in which an AI agent trades during the first 24 hours, is a possible explanation; this report has not verified it. Always query on-chain account state rather than hardcoding constants.

Three Graduation Archetypes

Type Examples Capital Flow Remaining Vulnerability
A Migrate to External DEX Early pump.fun (Raydium), four.meme Cross-protocol pool creation Pre-initialization exploits, off-chain relayers
B Atomic Migration to Native Pool Modern pump.fun, Pons V2 Atomic pool creation & LP lock Information shock at graduation
C Curveless Single-Sided Clanker, Zora, Flaunch, PAIR Zero asset migration Opening block sniping
  • Why Type C is Viable: Uniswap v4 relies on a singleton PoolManager, reducing pool creation to state modification; hooks can override dynamic fee structures inside beforeSwap, providing the primitive Clanker and Zora use for decaying taxes [8][9].
  • "In-Hook Curve with In-Place Phase Shift": Technically feasible. Hooks bearing beforeSwapReturnDelta permissions can intercept swap amounts and quote via arbitrary bonding curve formulas [8]. We did not identify a production launchpad using this architecture (speculative; search incomplete). Pons V2 utilizes a standalone curve contract that mints v4 positions upon graduation, placing it under Type B [18].
  • "v4 Yields Zero MEV" is False: Eliminating pool migration merely removes the migration execution gap. Front-running and sandwiching along the bonding curve remain mathematically unchanged.

Structural Defects and Design Axioms

F1

Asset Migration is the Primary Engineering Attack Surface in EVM Launchpads

confidence: high
Claim

four.meme was exploited twice (2025-02-11 and 2025-03-18); both incidents resulted from target-pool pre-initialization or contamination. Subsequent launchpads migrated atomically into proprietary pools or eliminated migration entirely.

Evidence

observed Verichains incident analysis: failure to validate sqrtPriceX96 on pre-existing pools caused ~$183K loss [10]. observed Second exploit abused deterministic pair address pre-funding [11]. observed 2025-03-31 architecture updated to PancakeSwap V2 migration with burned LP [12].

Implication

Never transfer funds into an external pool that allows permissionless initialization. Use Type B or Type C architectures (see K).

F2

Opening Sniping Originates from Curve Geometry; Protocols Can Only Mitigate It

confidence: high
Claim

Under classic curves, 53.65% of total supply is sold within the first 30 SOL net inflow. Protocols deploy delays, decaying taxes, wallet caps, and fixed-price windows to dampen sniping, but none eradicate it.

Evidence

inferred Derived from curve formulas (see price schedule in J) [1]. observed Clanker recommends an 80% → 5% decay over 30s [13]; Zora applies 99% over 10s [16]; Bankr combines 14s decay with 5-minute 2% wallet cap [25].

Implication

Anti-sniping parameters must match block production intervals. Faster, more deterministic block times allow finer tax decay steps.

F3

Perpetual Creator Revenue Splits Replaced One-Time Grants but Cannot Buy Retention

confidence: medium
Claim

pump.fun transitioned from 0.5 SOL graduation incentives to a perpetual 0.30% fee split; Zora, Pons, PAIR, and Bankr similarly distribute volume-based splits. When speculative volume wanes, creator earnings drop to zero.

Evidence

observed pump.fun 0.5 SOL grant in 2024-08 followed by trade fee splits starting 2025-05 [7][2]. observed Base App distributed ~$450K across 17K creators over 7 months ($26 average) [22]. inferred Zero quantitative evidence indicates fee sharing reduces insider self-dealing.

Implication

Embed revenue splits in contracts, but do not rely on them as a user retention engine.

F4

Multi-Quote Assets Fragment Liquidity without Production-Grade Balance Mechanisms

confidence: medium
Claim

Each quote token requires an independent pool without automated rebalancing. When stock tokens serve as quote assets, single meme pools can lock the majority of a tokenized stock's circulating supply, causing massive pricing distortions when underlying equity markets close.

Evidence

observed PAIR's AMC pool traded at 35x real market price over a weekend; the proposed peg guard would only pause new launches and had not yet launched [19]. observed A single HIMS pool held ~53% of on-chain tokenized HIMS supply (another study reports approximately 81%; the measurement bases differ).

Implication

Multi-quote setups require unified cross-routing and weekend circuit breakers; otherwise launch initially with a single quote asset.

F5

Subsidies and Exclusive Distribution Only Purchase Cold Starts

confidence: high
Claim

Base App sunset Creator Rewards, with leadership publicly acknowledging the failure of social creator tokens. Robinhood Chain's 90-day gas subsidy expired on 2026-09-29; launchpad activity declined afterward, according to secondary reporting. Printr similarly cited capital and distribution constraints.

Evidence

observed Base App sunset Creator Rewards in 2026-02; Jesse Pollak wrote on 2026-07-15 that creator coins "disintegrated completely … I was definitively wrong" [22]. observed Robinhood gas subsidy ran for 90 days, expiring 09-29 [21]. speculative Pons daily fees declined ~78% post-expiry (secondary reporting) [37]. observed Printr shutdown notice [24].

Implication

Evaluate KPIs by graduation counts and post-graduation token survival rates rather than gross token launches.

F6

Zero-Barrier Issuance Dilutes Individual Token Market Value

confidence: medium
Claim

When token deployment carries near-zero cost, supply expands unchecked via bots and automated agents, driving down average value per token.

Evidence

observed Pollak public reflection on social issuance failure [22]. speculative Average fee revenue per Clanker token dropped from $1,371 in 2024-12 to $53 in 2026-08 (unverified DefiLlama aggregate). inferred Bankr requires quota, wallet age, IP, and balance thresholds to curb sybil flooding [25].

Implication

Implement launch quotas or Sybil barriers before offering zero-fee agentic launches.

F7

Administrative Privileges Introduce Third-Party Trust Risk

confidence: medium
Claim

Even in audited contracts, actors holding withdrawal or configuration permissions represent critical vulnerabilities.

Evidence

observed 2024-05-16 pump.fun exploit where a former employee used withdrawal authority to siphon 12.3K SOL ($1.9M) [6]. observed Pons locker contract explicitly states "nobody, including protocol admin, can withdraw liquidity" [18].

Implication

Immutable parameters and recipient addresses must be hardcoded; any retained admin keys must be publicly verifiable on-chain.

Design Axioms Derived from Empirical Findings (all inferred):

  1. Avoid migration dependent on external pool creation: use single-sided LP or native in-pool graduation.
  2. Align anti-sniping parameters with block interval granularity, or adopt deterministic quota gates or auction-based gating.
  3. Code perpetual creator fees directly into contracts, but do not view them as retention guarantees.
  4. Multi-quote deployments require cross-pair routing and market-close circuit breakers.
  5. Subsidies only subsidize cold starts; optimize for token survival rates.
  6. Enforce immutable recipient routing in code and render admin privileges transparent.

F8

Existing Agentic Platforms Lack On-Chain Verifiable Permissions

confidence: high
Claim

Bankr enables agents to deploy and trade with limits, but controls run on proprietary servers. ElizaOS stores root private keys in plaintext environment variables. Clanker Droid prohibits agents from moving funds altogether. The core differentiator must be framed as "on-chain verifiable policy independent of custodial servers."

Evidence

observed Bankr defaults to $500 daily / $500 single-tx limits, with arbitrary calls enabled by default via off-chain policy [26]. observed Bankr explicitly warns that leaked agent keys can drain entire account balances [26]. observed ElizaOS mandates raw EVM_PRIVATE_KEY [29]. observed Droid docs [27].

Implication

Do not claim competitors lack spend limits; emphasize that limits are cryptographically enforced on-chain.

F9

"Zero-Barrier Launch, Strict Trading Limits" Bounds Losses but Cannot Prevent In-Whitelist Depletion

confidence: high
Claim

Issuance involves minimal inputs and deterministic branching, making it suitable for LLMs; trading involves slippage, MEV, and volatile pricing, requiring strict boundaries. While session keys prevent unauthorized asset transfers, an injected agent can still execute repeated unfavorable buys within whitelisted pools.

Evidence

observed product.md design tenet: "Zero-barrier launch, strict trading limits; place machine intelligence within a physical on-chain sandbox" [38]. observed Mainstream session modules default to sudo permissions unless explicit policies are attached [33]. inferred Bounding losses within whitelists requires drawdown caps or factory-token restrictions.

Implication

Always configure explicit expiry times and spend caps on sessions; incorporate maximum drawdown restrictions or restrict trading strictly to factory-issued tokens.

F10

"Fees Funding Inference" Has Been Empirically Validated Across Protocols

confidence: medium
Claim

Agent token issuers capture a portion of transaction fees to subsidize their own inference and gas costs. Clanker, Bankr, and Virtuals all employ this model; the proposed 70%–80% split is aligned with Virtuals.

Evidence

observed Clanker Droid allocates 10% of LP rewards [27]; Bankr creators receive 0.665% of volume [25]; Virtuals directs 70% of 1% tax [28]. observed Proposed design allocates ~1% fee, sending 70%–80% to agent runtime vaults [38].

Implication

Hardcode fee recipient vaults at token launch; restrict runtime vaults to quote assets to prevent human diversion.

Architecture Design: Permissions, Capital, and Chain Requirements

The following architecture reflects the product.md proposal; "proposed" denotes design specifications rather than deployed infrastructure [38].

Four Layers of Session Constraints (Proposed):

  1. Target Whitelist: Restrict execution strictly to LaunchpadFactory and Router; prohibit native transfer and arbitrary ERC-20 interactions.
  2. Selector Whitelist: Limit calls to launch(name, symbol), buyExactQuote, sellExactToken, and claimFee; prohibit arbitrary approve. Routers execute atomic approve-and-swap internally.
  3. Allowances: Enforce per-tx, per-block, and lifetime spend caps in quote denominations alongside strict slippage bounds.
  4. Expiry and Revocation: Implement mandatory time horizons (e.g., 24h auto-expiry) and instant on-chain revocation by the Owner.

These policies are implementable on ERC-7579 accounts via Rhinestone Smart Sessions, allowing simultaneous composition of call, spend, time, and usage policies [33].

  • Human Portal: Bybit AI Subaccounts launched 2026-05-27: isolated balances, zero master account exposure, API-only access [34]. Direct mapping to on-chain session keys remains an architectural proposal (speculative).
  • Agent Portal: Moltbook is an agent-only social network launched in 2026-01 and acquired by Meta in 2026-03 [35]. The architecture positions it as an optional discovery layer that complements direct API execution.

F11

Most Proposed Chain Requirements Can Be Bootstrapped on L2 Application Layers

confidence: medium
Claim

EIP-7702 is already active on Mantle mainnet; 2D nonces and Paymasters are standard in ERC-4337. Mantle should maintain execution on L2, only considering an L3 deployment if conditions F1–F3 are empirically proven on-chain.

Evidence

observed Mantlescan records ~60K EIP-7702 authorization transactions [36]. observed 4337 nonce structure and paymaster definitions [31]. inferred The claim that L2 cannot support 2D nonces only holds for raw EOAs and basic 7702 transactions. observed Documented bottlenecks: F1 hotspot spillover, F2 revert costs discouraging market makers, F3 co-located bot monopolies [38].

Implication

Prioritize testing production readiness of EntryPoints, Bundlers, and Paymasters on Mantle L2 (Open Question 1).

Contextual Constraints on Trade-offs:

  • Public documentation indicates byreal operates as a Bybit-incubated Solana DEX [36]; deploying byreal on Mantle to host v4 pools represents an unpublished proposal (speculative).
  • Bankr currently supports equity-denominated token launches across Robinhood Chain and Base [25], establishing external precedent for stock-quoted issuance.
  • "Bonding curves inside hooks" lacks production battle-testing (see K); adopting this route introduces custom engineering and audit overhead.

  • Open Questions:
