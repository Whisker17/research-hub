Options let people trade the shape of a payoff, not only its direction. Onchain options are limited by risk-bearing capacity, not by matching. Conclusion

Options are worth building, but Mantle should first add the risk-bearing layer, not an options exchange
  • Options turn protection and yield into products you can buy and sell. Someone on the sell side must carry jump risk (F1, F2).
  • Onchain options have solved matching but not risk bearing. A few venues hold most volume, and the total is small next to Deribit (F4, F5).

Background

B-0

Calls and puts

~3min
What it is

A call gives the buyer the right to buy the underlying at the strike K. A put gives the right to sell at K. The buyer pays a premium; the seller receives it and takes the obligation [1].

Why it matters here

Every structure and protocol on this page combines these two contracts.

Minimal example

A call with K = 2500 pays 500 if ETH is 3000 at expiry, and 0 if ETH is 2400.

Common misconception

Myth: buying a put is "shorting an option". Fact: buying a put is a long option position, used to bet on a fall or to protect spot.

Go deeper

[1] OIC, "Options Basics"

B-1

Contract specification

~3min
What it is

An option must state its underlying, strike, expiry time, exercise style, settlement method, multiplier and settlement-price rule. The holder can exercise a European option only at expiry, but can sell it before expiry [1][4].

Why it matters here

Most onchain option incidents hit the settlement-price path (fixing). RWAs add corporate-action rules.

Minimal example

Deribit ETH options are European with a multiplier of 1. They expire at 08:00 UTC and settle on a 30-minute TWAP of the index (07:30–08:00) [4].

Common misconception

Myth: the same underlying and strike make the same product. Fact: a different settlement-price source is a different risk.

Go deeper

[4] Deribit, "Linear USDC Options"

B-2

Implied volatility and Black-Scholes

~4min
What it is

Black-Scholes prices a European option from spot, strike, time, rate and volatility [8]. The volatility that makes the model match a market price is the implied volatility [2].

Why it matters here

Market makers in effect quote IV. IV and time to expiry set most of the premium.

Minimal example

ETH 2500, 30 days, IV 60%: the ATM call is $171.35. At IV 52% it falls to $148.55.

Common misconception

Myth: IV forecasts the future. Fact: IV is a parameter backed out of quotes. On 2026-10-15 ETH IV was about 52 and recent realized volatility about 44 [5].

Go deeper

[2] OIC, "Volatility & the Greeks"

B-3

Greeks and delta hedging

~4min
What it is

delta measures how much the option moves when the underlying moves $1. gamma measures how fast delta changes. theta is the time value lost per day. vega measures the price change for 1 point of IV [2].

Why it matters here

After selling an option, a market maker offsets delta with spot. The remaining gamma risk sets how much capital he needs.

Minimal example

The example ATM call: delta 0.534, gamma 0.000924, theta −$2.85/day, vega $2.85 per IV point.

Common misconception

Myth: delta-neutral means no risk. Fact: after a 10% jump, the hedged seller still loses about $27–30 (panel K).

Go deeper

[2] OIC, "Volatility & the Greeks"

B-4

Put-call parity

~3min
What it is

European options on the same underlying, strike and expiry satisfy C − P = S − PV(K) [8]. Calls, puts, spot and borrowing can replicate each other.

Why it matters here

This identity starts the World Markets paper. It also explains why a covered call equals a short put.

Minimal example

In the example, C2750 = 81.76 and P2750 = 331.76. The difference is −250 = 2500 − 2750.

Common misconception

Myth: spot plus lending can replicate an option. Fact: you still need a source of convexity: an opposite option or a claim with the same payoff [26].

Go deeper

[8] Stoll (1969)

B-5

Margin: full collateral and portfolio margin

~3min
What it is

Full collateral makes the seller lock the largest possible payout. Portfolio margin revalues the whole account across price and volatility scenarios and charges the worst loss [6][7].

Why it matters here

Full collateral is simple and safe but costs capital. Portfolio margin saves capital but depends on models and liquidation.

Minimal example

A 2500/2750 call spread pays at most $250, so locking $250 is enough. A naked short call has no maximum payout.

Common misconception

Myth: portfolio margin removes risk. Fact: it recognizes offsets but still depends on models and liquidation.

Go deeper

[7] Cboe, "Portfolio Margining"

Basics 1: Payoff Shapes and Common Structures

Options differ from spot and perps in payoff shape, not simply in leverage. Spot and perps draw straight lines: a $1 rise earns $1. Options draw bent lines: one side has a cap and the other stays open. Fig. 1 plots PnL at expiry with the example parameters.

Fig. 1 shows three points:

  • A long call loses at most $171.35, with uncapped upside. Its break-even is 2671.35, not 2500.
  • A long put is a long option position, but it earns money when price falls.
  • A short call earns at most $171.35, and its loss on a rally has no cap [1].

Alice's four choices: she holds 1 ETH and can reshape her payoff with options (Fig. 2).

Structure Max loss Max gain Use
Protective put (spot + long P2500) $171.35 Uncapped Insurance
Covered call (spot − C2750) $2,418.24 $331.76 Trade upside for income
Collar (spot + P2250 − C2750) $235.53 $264.47 Cheap insurance
Bull call spread 2500/2750 $89.59 $160.41 Cheap upside

The table values are expiry values from the OIC formulas [3].

Basics 2: Price, Implied Volatility and Greeks

Before expiry, option price = intrinsic value + time value. Fig. 3 shows the value of the K = 2500 call with different times left.

More time gives a higher, smoother curve. This extra value depends on IV and time left, and it decays each day as theta.

IV is a surface, not one number. Deribit ETH option quotes on 2026-10-15 [5]:

Contract IV Note
2000 put, Nov 27 56.4% Downside protection costs more (skew)
2400 call, Nov 27 49.7% Near the money
3000 call, Nov 27 52.5% Wings above the center (smile)
2400 call: Oct 30 / Nov 27 / Dec 25 46.5% / 49.7% / 50.6% Longer expiries are higher

At the same time, the ETH 30-day IV index DVOL was about 52, and recent realized volatility was about 44 [5]. The extra that option sellers collect pays for jump risk.

How Bob hedges: Bob sells Alice 1 ATM call for $171.35. He buys 0.534 ETH to offset delta. Then the price jumps at once:

Scenario New call price Bob option PnL Spot PnL Total
ETH → 2750 331.76 −160.41 +133.57 −26.84
ETH → 2250 67.29 +104.06 −133.57 −29.51

Bob loses in both directions: this is negative gamma. The approximation −½Γ(ΔS)² gives −$28.88. Bob collects about $2.85 of theta per day, so one 10% jump costs about 10 days of theta.

F2

The core risk of selling options is gamma: after a delta hedge, a 10% jump still costs $27–30

confidence: high
Claim

In the example, Bob's hedged position loses $26.84 and $29.51 on ±10% jumps, about 10 days of theta. Over a short interval Δt with IV unchanged, Bob's hedged PnL ≈ ½ΓS²[σ_IV²Δt − (ΔS/S)²], where Γ is the long option's positive gamma. The seller loses when realized variance exceeds implied variance.

The World Markets Paper: Mechanism

The paper is World Markets Research, "The Economics of Composable Options Markets" (2026-10, 12 pages) [26]. Its claim: an option's liquidity need not come only from that option's own order book.

The argument in four steps:

  1. Static replication: by parity, C = S + P − PV(K). Buy spot and the matching put, and fund PV(K) with a loan that repays K at expiry. The synthetic price is the sum of the legs; the depth is the smallest leg [26].
  2. A source of convexity: spot and ordinary loans supply only the spot and financing legs. You also need an opposite option or a maturity-settled non-recourse loan. At expiry, the lender of that loan receives K − max(K − S_T, 0).
  3. Dynamic replication + portfolio margin: without a static route, the maker sells the call the client wants. He buys a higher-strike call and hedges only the remaining delta with spot. A shared ledger counts term-lending claims at 98% toward margin [26][27].
  4. Estimate: if hedge options are available, October data support 532–1,052 quoted contracts. That is 5.9–11.7× the 90-contract historical best ask on Derive V2 [26].

The paper's capacity bound is Q≤min⁡(H/|Δ|, Ce/m, NR): hedge capacity, capital and residual-risk limit, whichever binds first [26].

F7

The paper's core idea holds: option liquidity can be assembled from spot, term financing and opposite options, and portfolio margin lets makers fund only residual risk

confidence: high
Claim

The scarcest leg limits each static route. A maker can sell a call spread instead of a naked call. Then the initial spot hedge falls from about 0.5 ETH per contract to 0.02–0.11 ETH. World's docs do state that lending claims count at 98% toward available margin.

Evidence

Implication

On a chain, options, lending and spot must share one margin ledger and support atomic multi-leg fills. "Composability" across separate protocols is not enough (W62).

F9

The engineering core of RWA options is clocks, units and permissions; they belong in the contract spec, not in operations

confidence: high
Claim

xStocks trade 24/7 onchain, while subscriptions and redemptions with the issuer run 24/5. Corporate actions take effect through the multiplier, and three sources give different activation times. The issuer contract can pause all transfers, so collateral locked in an option contract may fail to deliver. Ready-made parts exist: the ERC-4626 wrapper and the Chainlink v10 marketStatus and multiplier fields.

Glossary

callcall option
Gives the buyer the right to buy the underlying at the strike.not a synonym for "long option"
putput option
Gives the buyer the right to sell the underlying at the strike.buying a put is a long option
strikeK
The price at which the option buys or sells.not the spot price
premium
The price the buyer pays the seller for the option.not protocol revenue
European option
Can be exercised only at expiry; can be sold before expiry.unrelated to where it trades
fixingsettlement price
The price that sets the payout at expiry, such as a 30-minute TWAP.not the mark price
TWAPtime-weighted average price
The time-weighted average price over a window.
intrinsic value
What exercising now would pay, such as max(S − K, 0).
time value
Option price minus intrinsic value; it decays over time.
Black-ScholesBS model
A formula that prices European options from spot, strike, time, rate and volatility.Black-76 uses the forward instead of spot
implied volatilityIV
The volatility backed out of a market price through the model.not historical volatility
DVOL
Deribit's 30-day implied volatility index.
delta
First-order sensitivity of option price to the underlying price.
gamma
How fast delta changes as the underlying moves. Sellers are short gamma.
theta
Time value lost per day.
vega
Option price change for a 1-point move in IV.
convexity
A payoff that curves up: gains accelerate on rallies, losses have a floor.not leverage
Full collateralfully collateralized
The seller locks the largest possible payout; no liquidation.
Portfolio margin
Revalues the whole account across price and volatility scenarios and charges the worst loss.not isolated margin
DOVDeFi option vault
A vault that pools deposits and sells options on a schedule.not an options exchange
RFQrequest for quote
The user asks for a price, makers return signed quotes, and the user fills one onchain.not an order book
HIP-4outcome markets
Hyperliquid's primitive for fully collateralized contracts that settle within a range.not a vanilla option
put-call parity
C − P = S − PV(K).
multiplier
The factor xStocks use to reflect dividends, splits and fees.not the contract multiplier
