RESEARCH · USDT0 ON MANTLE · PRE-RESEARCH

Do Bybit users treat Mantle as a free bridge?

USDT0 flows on Mantle are large, and Bybit withdrawals to Mantle are free. That suggests a guess: users treat Mantle as a free bridge. This report tests the guess against 90 days of on-chain data, shows why it fails and why there is still a product worth building behind it, and ends with a product proposal and the decisions it needs.

PART 1

Background

First the things this report relies on: how USDT0 crosses chains, why bridging always costs native gas, why exchanges charge for withdrawals, and how we recognise Bybit on-chain. Then the observation and the hypothesis we test.

1What you need to know#

Which few things do you need to follow this report?

USDT0: one USDT, on many chains#

USDT0 is the omnichain version of Tether's USDT outside Ethereum. It follows LayerZero's OFT (Omnichain Fungible Token) standard: a lockbox contract on Ethereum (the OAdapter) holds real USDT, and USDT0 on every other chain is minted 1:1 against it. Moving from chain A to chain B burns on A and mints on B; moving back to Ethereum unlocks native USDT to the recipient. So it isn't a wrapped token: whoever receives it on Ethereum gets plain USDT.

Every crossing is a LayerZero message that three independent verifiers (DVNs) must all sign, LayerZero, USDT0 and Canary, before it executes on the destination. On Mantle the USDT0 token is 0x779d…3736 and the OFT contract that bridges it is 0xcb76…59cc.

Why bridging always costs the native coin#

Every transaction on any chain pays gas, and gas can only be paid in that chain's native coin: ETH on Ethereum, MNT on Mantle. An OFT transfer adds a LayerZero messaging fee for the verifiers and the destination execution, also paid in the source chain's native coin.

This is the first barrier (section 7 shows it isn't the only one)

Someone who has just withdrawn USDT0 from Bybit to Mantle holds USDT0 and no MNT. They can pay neither gas nor the LayerZero fee, so they can't move at all. The data will keep coming back to this.

Why exchanges charge for withdrawals, and why Mantle is free#

An exchange pays the gas to send your coins on-chain, so it charges a flat fee per network, usually above the real gas (it's also revenue). Ethereum has the most expensive gas and the highest fee. Bybit and Mantle are closely tied, and Bybit charges 0 for USDT0 withdrawals on Mantle. That is where this report starts.

2Observation and hypothesis#

What did we see, what did we guess, and what data could prove the guess wrong?

Observation. On the official USDT0 dashboard, Mantle's USDT0 inflow and outflow look strong, while Mantle's own DeFi and payments are not very active. That is a little odd: lots of money, few places to use it. And Bybit withdrawals to Mantle are free.

Hypothesis H. Users withdraw USDT0 from Bybit to Mantle for free, then bridge it over the OFT to the chain they really want (or the reverse: bridge into Mantle, then deposit into Bybit). Mantle is just a free bridge.

A good hypothesis can be refuted by data. If Mantle really were a bridge, it would show from three independent directions:

  1. P1 Looking forwardrecipients of Bybit withdrawals soon send the money out through the OFT.
  2. P2 Looking backOFT money leaving Mantle came mostly from Bybit withdrawals.
  3. P3 The reversemoney bridged into Mantle is soon deposited into Bybit.
  4. Pass markeach should be a sizeable share (tens of percent), not a rounding error.

Data and reasoning

Method and its limits first, then six findings. The first three answer "does the hypothesis hold?" (no); the last three answer "why does nobody take the route, does Mantle save money, and where does Bybit's money actually go?". Each finding carries an evidence grade: strong direct data, medium relies on heuristics or outside data, weak inference.

3Method#

Where does the data come from, how do we spot Bybit, and how do we tell where money "went"?

All data is public chain data on Dune: 16 SQL queries, all in the repository's queries/, that you can re-run as they are. Mantle analysis covers the last 90 days (2026-07-25 to 10-23); cross-chain Bybit flows 30 days, per-chain usage 7 days, Tron next hops 3 days.

  1. Pick the assettokens.erc20USDT0 on Mantle (0x779d…), about 746k transfers in 90 days. Legacy USDT is far smaller and out of scope.
  2. Spot BybitPoR · Q01Bybit's published proof-of-reserves address lists (Aug and Sep 2026) plus Dune labels: 31 addresses on Mantle, including all four hot wallets inferred earlier (and 0x5888…). On EVM chains an address has the same owner everywhere, so the PoR addresses of all chains are applied to every EVM chain.
  3. Define a crossingmantle.logsa transaction with the USDT0 OFT's OFTSent event is a crossing out; OFTReceived is a crossing in. Aggregators such as LI.FI that call the OFT count too.
  4. Follow the next hopQ03 · Q06for each withdrawal (or crossing in), take the recipient's next USDT0 transfer out and classify it: OFT, back to Bybit, DEX swap, Aave, other contract, plain address, not moved yet.
  5. Trace backQ05for each OFT send, take the sender's most recent USDT0 inflow before it: where did the money come from?
  6. SegmentQ03 · Q04three groups of recipients: whales (≥ $1M total), bots (≥ 200 withdrawals), retail (everyone else), so a few big players don't drown retail behaviour.
Limits of the method

Bybit addresses come from the official PoR (round 3). The PoR lists only reserve-holding addresses, not per-user deposit addresses, so deposits are measured by sweeps into PoR addresses: values are comparable, counts are not.

"Next hop" looks one step ahead (both Q03 and Q05). A user who first moves funds to another wallet of their own and bridges from there shows up as "to a plain address". Section 6 shows the conclusion survives even if all of that is bridging.

Round-3 correction. With the PoR list, the "$130M whale" of rounds 1–2 (0x98bedf…) turns out to be Bybit's own wallet, an internal transfer; real Bybit → Mantle withdrawals are corrected from $311.5M to $182.0M, retail numbers barely move. Fees now come from real Bybit app screenshots.

Round-2 correction. Round-1 queries double-counted because mantle.contracts has several rows for some addresses (LI.FI has 6). Every number on this page is the corrected one (revision log in the appendix).

No large-scale arbitrage

All Mantle DEXs together traded only about $110M in 90 days (about $1.2M a day), and the market maker is 27% of it. Market making at that scale can't explain $400M of two-way flow; the balance comes mostly from a yield product's subscriptions and redemptions.

Why so much money goes to Aave: Aave needs no market making

What flows into Aave is yield capital, not market-making money: Bybit's on-chain yield product Mantle Vault (its strategy contract is Aave's second-largest USDT0 supplier on Mantle, $58.7M supplied in 90 days), and large holders bridging from Ethereum to earn Aave yield (section 4).

Why it matters to us

Bybit already runs two products on Mantle: on-chain yield (Mantle Vault) and on-chain trading (Alpha, inferred). Bybit is willing to use Mantle as an execution layer for its products, and Alpha's trades on Mantle are a ready-made order-flow entry for the JIT Router / Prop AMM (section 18, ⑥).

Recommended MVP: a forwarding deposit address (Form A)#

The user enters a destination chain, a recipient and the asset they want, and gets their own Mantle address. Every Bybit withdrawal on the Mantle network to that address is forwarded to the destination automatically.

  • The user signs nothing on Mantle, pays no gas, never touches MNT. The most complete form of "gasless".
  • It fits Bybit's existing withdrawal flow: save the address in the Bybit address book and reuse it.
  • The address comes from CREATE2 with the destination baked in, so nobody can change where the money goes (section 15).
  • Direct competitor: rhino.fi Smart Deposit Addresses already run on Mantle, essentially Form A (section 7; inferred from on-chain patterns, to confirm against rhino.fi's published contracts in V7). NEAR Intents 1Click and Relay have similar deposit addresses.

So decide build vs partner first. A′: partner with or white-label rhino.fi and spend our effort on distribution (Bybit) and Mantle-side value (gas drop, Prop AMM). A: build it, differentiated by the native OFT (1:1, zero spread, native USDT on Ethereum), Mantle's official standing and Bybit integration. Do V7 first: compare rhino.fi's fees, limits and destinations.

15System architecture#

What are the parts, and how does money get from Bybit to Ethereum?

  1. Destination baked into the addressCREATE2 salt = hash(destination chain, recipient, destination asset, refund address). The Relayer can trigger forwarding but can't redirect. Deployed lazily on first use (EIP-1167 minimal proxy).
  2. Fees in USDT0the Router takes LZ quote + Mantle gas + a margin at execution; the Relayer fronts MNT and is repaid in USDT0.
  3. Refunds, to a separate addressbelow minimum, route unavailable (NoPeer, limits) or a screening hit → refund address. It must not default to the destination: if that is another exchange's Ethereum deposit address or a contract wallet, a refund to the same address on Mantle is lost. Or hold funds in the Forwarder until the user claims them with a signature. Stray MNT, legacy USDT and other assets go back via rescue().
  4. Replayable and recoverableflush() forwards the whole balance, so later deposits are forwarded too. Anyone can call flush() (a Relayer outage can't trap funds), but the caller brings the MNT, the fee is computed on-chain from quoteSend rather than supplied by the caller, and the caller reward is capped against fee extraction.

16Choosing the bridge layer: LayerZero OFT or NEAR Intents#

Intents let the user state what they want and a market maker (solver) fills it from its own inventory on the destination: any asset, but dependent on solver quotes and inventory.

Glossary#

USDT0
the omnichain version of Tether's USDT: native USDT locked on Ethereum, minted 1:1 elsewhere.
OFT
Omnichain Fungible Token, LayerZero's cross-chain token standard: burn on the source, mint on the destination.
LayerZero / DVN
a cross-chain messaging protocol; DVNs are independent verifiers, and USDT0 requires all three to sign.
LZ (messaging) fee
the fee paid in the source chain's native coin when sending a crossing: MNT on Mantle.
Hot wallet / deposit address
the address an exchange sends withdrawals from / the per-user address it receives on, swept into the hot wallet.
EOA
an ordinary private-key account (as opposed to a contract account).
AA (account abstraction)
programmable accounts (sponsored gas, batching, permissions), e.g. ERC-4337, EIP-7702.
permit / EIP-3009
off-chain signed token approvals: the user signs, someone else submits and pays gas.
Relayer / paymaster
a service / contract that submits transactions and fronts gas for users.
CREATE2
a deployment method whose address is known before deployment; parameters in the salt bind the address to them.
Intents / solver
the user states the outcome they want; a market maker (solver) delivers it from its own inventory.
Prop AMM
a proprietary market-making AMM quoting from the maker's pricing engine and inventory rather than a passive pool.
JIT Router
an aggregator with just-in-time liquidity, injected at execution time.
bps
basis points, 0.01%.
