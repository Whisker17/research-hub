Uniswap v4 Hooks: Mechanics, v3 Feasibility, RWA, and In-Pool Graduation

The key distinction of v4 is linking programmable execution rules directly into unified settlement. PoolManager guarantees only net balance clearance; developers must independently prove their hook's economic incentives, regulatory boundaries, and routability.

Bottom line: The true differentiator of v4 hooks is not merely executing arbitrary code around swaps. It lies in hooks actively determining trade sizing while composing natively with concentrated liquidity, multi-hop net settlement, and ERC-6909 internal claims (F2). Gating rules and dynamic fees become universally binding across all callers only when codified at the hook layer (F1). Crucially, PoolManager verifies only that per-address, per-currency net balances clear to zero at the end of unlock, without verifying whether a hook's internal accounting is economically sound (F3). For real-world assets (RWA), access gating belongs at the pool layer, but pool rules cannot substitute for token-layer transfer compliance (F4). Single-pool graduation for equity-quoted launchpads is mathematically viable, but lacks production precedents (F6).

Conclusions

Master settlement semantics before writing hooks
  • Mechanics: Hooks can reject calls, override fees, and modify accounting; net debt must clear before unlock ends, but zero net debt does not guarantee correct internal accounting.
  • v3 Comparison: Callbacks themselves do not strictly require v4; what truly requires v4 is the singleton architecture, flash accounting, and return deltas.
  • RWA: Place whitelist gating at the pool level, while preserving compliance restrictions at the token level; permissioned pools disallow permissioned tokens from acting as intermediate hops.
  • In-Pool Graduation: Mathematically sound; key engineering hurdles are settlement price, reserve solvency, position conversion, and crossing-order execution. Prove concentrated multi-curve LP is insufficient before adding custom curves.

B-0

v3 Swap Callback Payments

~5min
What it is

In Uniswap v3, the pool transfers output tokens to the recipient first, then calls uniswapV3SwapCallback on the caller, expecting payment before checking its resulting balance.

Why it matters here

This callback targets the caller rather than an independent pool policy; anyone can implement the callback and interact with the pool directly, bypassing front-end routers.

Common misconception

Misconception: The v3 callback functions like a hook. Fact: Callbacks are caller payment channels; v3 core provides zero universal hooks binding all callers.

Concentrated and Single-Sided Liquidity

~5min
What it is

LPs allocate capital within a bounded tick range. When the range lies entirely above or below current price, the position holds only a single token.

Why it matters here

Many curveless issuance designs place newly issued tokens into single-sided or multi-range concentrated LP positions to mimic bonding curves. This pattern is fully achievable on v3.

Common misconception

Misconception: Curveless issuance requires v4. Fact: Emulating curves via static concentrated liquidity positions works identically on v3.

Transient Storage (EIP-1153)

~5min
What it is

TSTORE and TLOAD read and write transient storage valid only for the duration of the current transaction, clearing automatically at gas cost of 100 per operation.

Why it matters here

Uniswap v4 logs lock status and per-currency net deltas inside transient storage, asserting clearance only at unlock termination.

Common misconception

Misconception: EIP-1153 is a prerequisite for KYC checks. Fact: It is the operational foundation for v4 flash accounting, independent of identity logic.

B-3

ERC-6909 Claims

~5min
What it is

Multi-token balance vouchers internal to PoolManager. Users and hooks can claim owed tokens inside PoolManager without transferring ERC-20s, burning claims later to settle debits.

Why it matters here

High-frequency market makers and hooks avoid external ERC-20 transfer overhead; transfers of 6909 claims occur invisibly to the underlying ERC-20 contract.

Common misconception

Misconception: ERC-6909 claims represent LP shares. Fact: Claims correspond to raw token balances without price ranges or pool association.

CREATE2 Address Mining (HookMiner)

~4min
What it is

Contract deployment address is deterministically computed from deployer, salt, and initcode hash. Salt values are mined until the lower 14 bits match the required permission flags.

Why it matters here

PoolManager checks the lower 14 bits of the hook address rather than an on-chain registry to determine callback invocations.

Common misconception

Misconception: Permissions can be adjusted post-deployment. Fact: Address bits define permissions; changing permissions requires deploying a new address and initializing a new pool.

J v4 Mechanics: Singleton, Unlock, and Flash Accounting

  • Singleton Architecture: All pools exist as state entries _pools[PoolId] inside PoolManager. PoolId is computed by hashing the PoolKey struct (currency0, currency1, fee, tickSpacing, hooks). Creating a pool writes storage without deploying bytecode [1].
  • One Hook per Pool: Each pool binds exactly one hook address. A single hook contract can serve multiple pools, keeping state partitioned by PoolId [1][8].
  • Hooks Are External Calls: PoolManager enters hooks via external calls. A hook interacts with execution in four ways: reverting the call, returning a dynamic fee, returning a delta override, or calling PoolManager during unlock [2].

Flash Accounting and Delta Clearance

F1

Pool-Layer Enforcement Binds All Callers, Eliminating Router-Level Bypass Vectors

confidence: high
Claim

Rules encoded into v4 hooks execute on every swap, liquidity action, and donation across all external callers (with the exception of hook self-calls permitted by v4-core). In v3, custom router rules are bypassed if callers interact directly with the pool.

Evidence

observed PoolManager execution flow in PoolManager.sol#L104-L140 [1].

Implication

All access-control and pricing policies must reside in hooks to be universally binding.

F2

Flash Accounting and Return Deltas Enable Custom Automated Market Maker Curves Inside Pools

confidence: high
Claim

Hooks bearing beforeSwapReturnDelta can absorb trades, overriding native concentrated liquidity calculation and quoting via arbitrary formulas.

Evidence

observed v4-core Hooks.sol bitflag BEFORE_SWAP_RETURNS_DELTA_FLAG [1][8].

Implication

Bespoke bonding curves can operate inside standard Uniswap v4 pools without separate curve deployment.

F3

PoolManager Guarantees Balance Clearance but Not Economic Solvency

confidence: high
Claim

PoolManager checks only that NonzeroDeltaCount == 0 upon unlock exit. It does not validate whether a hook's pricing formula is fair or whether its reserves are solvent.

Evidence

observed PoolManager.sol#L111 assert statement [1].

Implication

Hook developers bear complete responsibility for the economic and cryptographic security of custom accounting.

Compliance, RWA, and Permissioned Pools

F4

Pool Access Allowlists Cannot Replace Token-Level Transfer Restrictions for Regulated RWAs

confidence: high
Claim

While a hook can restrict swaps and LP actions to KYC-verified addresses, traders receiving underlying ERC-20 tokens can transfer them outside the pool unless the token contract itself enforces compliance.

Evidence

observed Architecture of Uniswap Permissioned Pools standard [23].

Implication

Regulated assets must implement transfer restrictions at both the token contract and the pool hook layer.

F5

Official Permissioned Pools Prevent Permissioned Tokens from Acting as Intermediate Routing Hops

confidence: medium
Claim

Uniswap's Permissioned Pool implementation disallows permissioned tokens from being used as intermediate bridging hops in multi-hop swaps, protecting unqualified accounts from receiving restricted assets.

Evidence

observed PermissionedPoolHook.sol transfer validation logic [23].

Implication

Multi-hop routing algorithms must filter out compliance-restricted token pairs for general public orderflow.

In-Pool Graduation and Launchpad Mechanics

F6

In-Pool Phase Shifts Are Mathematically Sound but Lack Production Deployment Precedents

confidence: high
Claim

A hook quoting a bonding curve via return deltas can mathematically shift into native concentrated liquidity upon hitting reserve thresholds. However, zero major production launchpads currently adopt this architecture.

Evidence

observed Survey of active launchpads on Base, Solana, and Robinhood Chain; Pons V2 and Clanker utilize separate deployment or curveless models [30][31][33].

Implication

Teams adopting in-pool graduation must conduct novel security audits on phase transition boundaries.

F7

Crossing Orders Straddling Graduation Require Deterministic Splitting

confidence: medium
Claim

When a single swap spans across the curve-to-concentrated liquidity threshold, the hook must either split execution between curve and concentrated ticks or clamp the fill at the threshold.

Evidence

inferred Discrete execution analysis on bonding curve phase shifts.

Implication

Clamping and refunding excess input is significantly simpler and safer than multi-engine execution within a single transaction.

Security Case Studies and Post-Mortems

F8

Cork Protocol Exploit: Unvalidated hookData Created Free Flash Lending

confidence: high
Claim

Cork Protocol's hook accepted caller-supplied hookData without asserting origin authority, allowing an attacker to drain ~$12M by forging liquidity parameters.

Evidence

observed Post-mortem analysis of Cork exploit on 2025-05 [15].

Implication

Never trust caller-supplied hookData without verifying signatures or strict caller whitelists.

F9

Bunni Protocol Exploit: Multi-Step Rounding Truncation Led to Precision Drain

confidence: high
Claim

Bunni Protocol suffered an ~$8.4M drain due to integer rounding discrepancies between multi-step liquidity operations within a single unlock session.

Evidence

observed Bunni Protocol post-mortem and subsequent operational shutdown [16].

Implication

Hook math must round against the caller on both deposit and withdrawal paths.

F10

Pool Reentrancy and Hook Recursion Require Guard Locks

confidence: medium
Claim

PoolManager prevents nested unlocks, but hooks that invoke external contracts or other pools can create indirect reentrancy loops unless reentrancy guards are applied.

Evidence

observed Lock.sol single-unlock assertion [1].

Implication

Hooks must enforce non-reentrant guards on state-modifying callbacks.
