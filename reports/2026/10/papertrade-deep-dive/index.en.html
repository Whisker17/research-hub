Papertrade Deep Dive: Vault-Counterparty Perps, Loss-Based Minting & Mantle Feasibility

Papertrade combines single-orderbook mid quoting, protocol vault counterparty betting, winner-only fees, and loss-based token minting. Early traction stemmed from launch pulse and loss cashback; the exploit was enabled by a ~$100B per-side launch OI cap. The mechanics can be replicated on Mantle; missing primitives are native on-chain price reads and native USDC, while the true constraint is organic demand.

Bottom line: Papertrade is a synthetic perpetual protocol quoting Hyperliquid BBO mid-prices with protocol LP acting as the direct counterparty. It charges fees strictly on winning profits and mints PAPER tokens exclusively to losing traders (F1, F2). In its advertised "zero-capital launch," queuing credit was only utilized for $22.86. Protocol LP reached $5M within 65 minutes, after which it was capped at ~$5M by diverting excess earnings to stakers (F3). Initial volume was a standard launch pulse; staker USDC payouts equaled ~80% of net trader losses, effectively returning cash to losing accounts (F5).

The root cause of the exploit was an on-chain launch OI cap of ~$100B per side rather than the documented "current OI + $1B" (F7). For Mantle, the ledger, payout queues, and tokenomics can be ported as-is. Chain-level gaps are limited to native on-chain price precompiles and native USDC / CCTP. The true hurdle is sustainable market demand (F11, F12).

Conclusions

Port mechanics to Mantle, but do not copy its pricing or risk parameters
  • Papertrade's novelty lies in capital structure (zero external LP, queue, loss minting) and pricing (single BBO mid). The haircut model adapts Rollbit (F2, F9).
  • "Zero-capital launch" lasted only minutes, subsequently sustained by first-hour trader losses and a $5M vault cap (F3).
  • Pricing lacks deviation circuit breakers while launch caps were 100x too wide, directly enabling the exploit (F6, F7).
  • Replicating on Mantle: adopt commit-reveal signed price reports; size caps from LP risk budgets; prioritize liquidations via maintenance lanes (F13). Validate organic demand before building.

B-0

HyperCore and Read Precompiles

~3min
What it is

HyperCore is Hyperliquid's consensus layer running high-performance orderbooks. HyperEVM contracts invoke dedicated precompiles via staticcall to read the latest HyperCore state directly at block construction time [5].

Why it matters here

Papertrade reads prices directly on-chain without relying on off-chain oracle signers.

Minimal example

0x...080e takes asset ID (BTC = 0) and returns (uint64 bid, uint64 ask) costing ~8,240 gas [5][8].

Common misconception

Misconception: Precompiles stream continuous real-time prices. Fact: EVM contracts read a discrete snapshot once per EVM block (~1s blocks) [6].

B-1

Vault-Counterparty Synthetic Perpetuals

~3min
What it is

Synthetic perpetuals do not match buyers and sellers. The protocol logs entry price and settles PnL upon close directly against a vault counterparty [1].

Why it matters here

Zero slippage and zero spread arise from this structure since the protocol does not hedge externally.

Minimal example

Alice's $100,000 notional position is purely an on-chain promise paid out by the LP vault.

Common misconception

Misconception: Unhedged positions never charge funding. Fact: GMX v2 and gTrade operate synthetic vaults while charging dynamic funding/borrow fees [14][17].

B-2

BBO, Mid-Price, and Mark Price

~3min
What it is

BBO denotes best bid and offer. mid is their arithmetic midpoint. mark price is Hyperliquid's median-of-three liquidation price [7].

Why it matters here

Papertrade executes opens, closes, and liquidations against mid-price, whereas Hyperliquid itself never uses mid-price for liquidations.

Minimal example

On 10-12, BTC orderbook quoted $82,513.0 / $82,514.0, yielding mid = $82,513.5 (1 tick spread ≈ 0.12 bps) [3].

Common misconception

Misconception: Consensus-attested prices represent fair value. Fact: Consensus only proves that a quote existed on that specific orderbook [1].

B-3

Martingaler Payout Queue

~4min
What it is

A FIFO queue recording unpaid winning profits. When the LP vault lacks liquidity, profits queue until future trader losses refill the pool [2].

Why it matters here

Provides the theoretical backing for Papertrade's "zero-capital launch."

Minimal example

During the first 6 seconds, three winners earned $7.62 each while LP was zero, queuing until the next trader loss cleared them [3].

Common misconception

Misconception: The queue mathematically guarantees ultimate solvency. Fact: Whitepapers simulate toy probabilistic games without proving asymptotic solvency [2].

B-5

OI Cap and Net Exposure

~3min
What it is

OI cap limits cumulative open position size per side. Net exposure = Long Notional - Short Notional, determining directional vault risk.

Why it matters here

Without funding rates to rebalance skew, Papertrade relies entirely on OI caps to constrain drawdown risk.

Minimal example

On 10-10 at 15:00, traders held $1.23B net long while LP was $4.2M; a 0.34% concurrent BTC/ETH rally would have depleted the vault [4].

Payout Haircut Schedule

For profitable trades, the net payout formula is:

Payout=Notional×Δ′×scale×0.98

where price movement after deadband is Δ′=move−0.2 bps, and the scale factor is defined as:

scale=0.91+115,000⋅Δ′+105106⋅814.6⋅Δ′

confidence: high
Claim

At 0.05%, 0.1%, and 1% price moves, BTC profits retain only 60.7%, 72.4%, and 86.4% respectively, while ETH retains 54.0%, 67.6%, and 85.7%. The model adapts Rollbit's haircut curve.

Implication

High leverage profits are heavily dampened on small moves, increasing the cost of short-term price manipulation.

The PAPER minting formula scales inversely with cumulative historical LP growth H. When LP is below $2M, 100 PAPER is minted per $1 loss; thereafter:

Mantle Feasibility and Recommendations

F8

Mantle Lacks Native L1 Orderbook State Precompiles

confidence: medium
Claim

Mantle does not run an integrated L1 orderbook consensus. Fetching low-latency prices requires signed pull oracles.

Evidence

observed Mantle architecture specifications and opcode tables [25].

Implication

A direct copy of HyperCore precompile quoting cannot function on Mantle.

F9

Commit-Reveal Pull Oracles Introduce Front-Running and Stale-Report Vectors

confidence: medium
Claim

Using pull oracles on L2 requires two-phase commit-reveal ordering to prevent keepers from cherry-picking favorable price timestamps.

Evidence

inferred Oracle delay analysis across Pyth and Chainlink Data Streams [23][24].

Implication

Orders must be committed before price reports are unveiled.

Replication is Structurally Feasible but Constrained by Demand Rather than Technology

Claim

Contracts, queues, and tokenomics can be deployed on Mantle with standard EVM tools. However, Mantle's derivative TVL is limited compared to Hyperliquid.

F12

Risk Caps Must Scale as a Function of Vault Equity and Liquidation Latency

confidence: high
Claim

Maximum allowable net position caps must scale strictly with vault equity to guarantee that single-block adverse moves cannot drain the pool.

Evidence

inferred VaR simulation models under 1000x leverage constraints.

Implication

Never set static multi-billion dollar caps.

F13

Liquidations Require Priority Maintenance Lanes on the Sequencer

confidence: medium
Claim

High-leverage liquidations have narrow margin buffers (e.g. 5.24 bps at 1000x). If liquidation transactions are delayed behind user traffic, bad debt accumulates.

Evidence

observed Bust buffer calculations under 1000x leverage schedules.

Implication

Liquidations must route through sequencer priority maintenance lanes.
