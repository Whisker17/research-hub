Conclusions

Three Strict Constraints for Mantle
  1. JIT Routers must query quotes and route swaps within the execution transaction. Section 9 outlines this exact requirement.
  2. Fast lanes must strictly permit price updates, never fee-tier updates. Tessera's spoofing relies on paired default-fee updates at block start and block end.
  3. Track KPIs via markout and quote-execution consistency, not quoted spreads alone. Only 39% of Tessera trades execute at quoted rates.

Background & Motivation

Problem: Passive AMM quotes move only when someone trades against them. When external market prices shift, arbitrageurs pick off stale quotes, leaving LPs to absorb LVR. The paper measures passive AMM losses at 0.22–2.06 bps two seconds post-trade (F1).

Why Now: propAMMs have become dominant liquidity venues. By 2026, they capture over half of Solana SOL/USDC volume. Market share on Base and Monad continues to climb. Mantle is designing its own RFQ / propAMM and Router, requiring empirical insight into where venue profits come from and who pays for them.

Paper Position:

  • Not a SoK. Its value lies in primary empirical data: 3 chains, 2025-09-01 through 2026-08-31 swaps, state updates, and Bybit order books.
  • It completely reverse-engineers closed contracts: Tessera fully reconstructed, Metric and Elfomo partially decoded.
  • It converts industry rumors (counterparty-specific pricing, spoofing) into verifiable metrics.

Scope: Solana SOL/USDC, Base ETH/USDC, Monad MON/USDC. Non-goals: Ethereum mainnet propAMMs (viewed by authors as builder-coordinated, closer to RFQ), on-chain CLOBs (like Kuru), long-tail assets.

Prerequisites

B-0

Loss-Versus-Rebalancing (LVR)

~6min
What it is

The expected loss suffered by passive liquidity providers against informed arbitrageurs whenever external market reference prices move.

Why it matters here

Passive AMM curves cannot proactively update prices; they only adjust when an external trade hits them.

Minimal example
CEX price jumps $2,000 → $2,020.
Arbitrageur buys from the AMM at stale $2,000 price and sells on CEX, taking $20 from LP equity.
Common misconception

Misconception: LVR is identical to impermanent loss. Fact: Impermanent loss assumes path independence; LVR accumulates continuously on price volatility even if price returns to start.

B-1

Markout

~8min
What it is

The difference between trade execution price and external Bybit microprice reference after a fixed delay τ (e.g., τ=2s). Positive markout means the maker earned alpha relative to post-trade market price.

Why it matters here

Markout directly isolates whether the orderflow received was toxic (informed) or benign (uninformed).

Minimal example
Taker buys ETH at $2,000.
At t + 2s, CEX mid-price is $1,998.
The maker sold high relative to subsequent price: Markout is +10 bps.
Common misconception

Misconception: Markout equals net PnL. Fact: Markout is gross alpha before gas, hedging slippage, and inventory holding costs.

B-2

Proactive Market Maker (propAMM)

~7min
What it is

An on-chain liquidity pool whose quoting curves are proactively updated by an off-chain market maker submitting state transactions.

Why it matters here

Allows active makers to pull quotes and update prices as CEX prices shift, avoiding adverse selection from passive stale quotes.

Minimal example
Tessera updates anchor price p and spread s via 25-byte state updates on Base.
Common misconception

Misconception: propAMM is a fully on-chain order book. Fact: It quotes via parametrized algorithmic curves whose parameters are dynamically updated.

Intra-Block Transaction Ordering

~6min
What it is

The deterministic sequence in which transactions execute within a single block or sub-block, decided by sequencers or builders.

Why it matters here

On Base Flashblocks, 200ms sub-blocks allow market makers to insert quote updates between user trades.

Common misconception

Misconception: Transactions in the same block execute with identical state. Fact: State updates change pricing dynamically between transactions.

Snapshot-Based Off-Chain Routing

~6min
What it is

Decentralized aggregators (e.g., 0x, 1inch) simulating execution off-chain via eth_call against state snapshots to find the cheapest route.

Why it matters here

Creates an information gap: user trades execute against later intra-block state where fees may have been adjusted.

Common misconception

Misconception: Off-chain quotes guarantee on-chain execution prices. Fact: State can diverge prior to block execution.

Core Findings

F1

propAMM Generates Positive Markout While Passive AMMs Suffer Negative Markout

confidence: high
Claim

Across Solana, Base, and Monad over one year, propAMM markout 2 seconds post-trade averages +0.37 to +1.69 bps, while passive AMMs average -0.22 to -2.06 bps.

Evidence

observed Paper Table 1, Figures 4 and 5 [1]. observed Base Tessera achieves positive markout across 91% of trading days.

Implication

Active quoting successfully neutralizes LVR on high-frequency rollups.

F2

Low-Cost State Updates Outpace Taker Execution

confidence: high
Claim

Updating quotes via lightweight calldata costs as low as ~26.7k gas on Base. Tessera updates anchor quotes once per 0.563 blocks.

Evidence

observed RPC verification of Base blocks 50,369,777 and 50,196,022 [1].

Implication

Priority lanes for quote updates protect makers from adverse selection.

F3

Counterparty-Specific Pricing Enforces Tiered Penalty Schedules

confidence: high
Claim

Tessera inspects tx.origin and calling contracts via bytecode fingerprinting, levying up to 6 additive penalty fees (ϕ).

Evidence

observed Decompiled fingerprint contract 0xfdb7...e684 [1].

Implication

Routers must query executable prices inside transactions rather than relying on off-chain simulations.

F4

Penalties Account for Significant Effective Execution Costs

confidence: high
Claim

While nominal quoted fees are ~0.44 bps, volume-weighted realized fees reach 1.95 bps once penalty components are factored in.

Evidence

observed Appendix C.1 mathematical formulation and fee accounting [1].

Implication

Fee components must be exposed programmatically to maintain execution transparency.

F5

propAMMs Win Cross-Venue Atomic Arbitrage Against Passive AMMs

confidence: medium
Claim

In atomic arbitrage across propAMM and passive AMM pairs, the propAMM leg breaks even or achieves positive alpha, while the passive AMM leg absorbs losses.

Evidence

observed Paper Table 2 and Figure 6(b) [1].

Implication

Introducing propAMMs increases the adverse selection experienced by remaining passive AMM LPs.

F6

Spoofing: Only 39% of Trades Execute at the Quoted Snapshot Price

confidence: high
Claim

Tessera raises default fees near the beginning of blocks and resets them near block ends. Realized trades execute 1.08 bps worse than end-of-block quotes.

Evidence

observed Paper Section 7.4, Table 3, and Figure 7 [1]. observed 0x independent report finding 97.4% of final updates in the last sub-block [4].

Implication

Sequencers must prevent fee-rate updates from entering fast pre-confirmation channels.

F7

Retail Traders Execute at Lower Nominal Cost on propAMMs

confidence: medium
Claim

During calm market periods (reference price moving < 1 bp over 6 seconds), orderflow pays 0.26 to 1.62 bps on propAMMs versus 1.38 to 8.60 bps on passive AMMs.

Evidence

observed Paper Section 8 and Figure 8 [1]. inferred Calm trades serve as a proxy for retail, without independent identity telemetry.

Implication

Lower retail spreads serve as a genuine product advantage when paired with protections against spoofing.

F8

Quote Updates Are Submitted Concurrently as 25-Byte Payloads

confidence: medium
Claim

Updates bypass standard ABI function selectors, packing sequence number, reference block, anchor price, and spread into 25 bytes.

Evidence

observed Base RPC analysis of blocks 50,369,777 and 50,196,022 [1].

Implication

Sequencers can prune outdated sequence updates from the same maker, saving execution gas.

Detailed Mathematical Formulation

The overall swap execution formula is:

Yexec=Ybook(x)×(1−Φ),Φ=∑iϕi106(i∈{base, age, priority, mode, default, address})

where each penalty component ϕi is expressed in parts-per-million (ppm).

ceff=104⋅pa−pb(1−Φ)2pa+pb(1−Φ)2

and the portion deducted by the k-th penalty term corresponds to:

ΔYk=Y⋅ϕk106−∑ϕi

Implications for Mantle

JIT Router Principles:

  1. Execute quote() and compare net output dynamically inside the execution transaction.
  2. Require quote() and swap() to share identical penalty logic, asserting execution is no worse than quote.
  3. Pre-agree on router classification with market makers to avoid generic router penalty tagging.
  4. Support Fluxion RFQ quotes alongside on-chain propAMM pools.

Sequencer & Protocol Rules:

  1. Fast lanes must strictly permit pricing updates (p, s, sequence cancellation). Fee-tier and access-control updates must follow normal block sequencing.
  2. Parameter changes affecting taker fees must apply with an activation delay of at least one block to prevent decoy quoting.
